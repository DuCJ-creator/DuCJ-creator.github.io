01 • The foundation

From noticing to insight

Excellence is not a longer plot summary. It is a chain of reasoning that explains how a deliberate choice constructs meaning and why that meaning matters.

Use for every text type

Build every analytical paragraph in five moves

TTopicMake a direct argument that answers the prompt.
EEvidenceSelect a precise quote, shot, sound, or structural detail.
EExplainIdentify the technique and its literal meaning.
AAnalyseUnpack connotations, subtext, and audience impact.
LLinkExplain creator purpose and the wider human idea.
The excellence test
“So what does this choice help the audience understand?”

If your answer reaches the creator’s view of human behaviour, society, or the world, you are analysing rather than retelling.

02 • Written text

Ideas, people, worlds, patterns

Track what changes, what repeats, what clashes, and what the author’s choices reveal beneath the surface.

Novel • Story • Poetry • Drama
Central ideas

Themes

A theme is the author’s underlying statement about human behaviour, society, or the world—not a one-word topic.

  • Identity & belonging: cultural conflict, self-acceptance, finding one’s place
  • Power & corruption: oppression, resistance, class, institutional control
  • Courage & resilience: adversity, moral courage, endurance
  • Coming of age: loss of innocence and transition to adulthood
  • Isolation vs community: prejudice, loneliness, unity
Move from topic to argument

Instead of “the theme is conformity,” write: “The text reveals that blind compliance with social norms can destroy individual integrity.”

Character

Characterisation

How personality and motivation are revealed and developed.

  • Direct vs indirect revelation
  • Dynamic vs static character arc
  • Internal desire vs external pressure
  • Relationships and contrasting foils
  • Pivotal turning points
Ask this

What changes—and what action, relationship, or pressure causes that change?

World-building

Setting

Physical place, time, and social atmosphere work as active meaning-makers.

  • Physical: landscape, climate, boundaries
  • Temporal: period, era, season, time of day
  • Social: cultural norms, politics, class
Three functions

Setting may catalyse conflict, mirror a character’s psychology, or become a symbolic space.

Narrative mechanics

Conflict & plot structure

Conflict: person vs person, self, society, or nature.

  • Exposition → baseline world
  • Inciting incident → disruption
  • Rising action → complications
  • Climax → point of no return
  • Falling action & resolution → consequences
Structural devices

In medias res, flashbacks, foreshadowing, and parallel narratives can reshape what the audience knows and when.

Language features

03 • Visual text

Everything in frame is a choice

Film meaning lives in image, sound, performance, space, and sequence. Connect technical detail to subtext and director intent.

Film • Documentary • Moving image
Intent & subtext

The idea beneath the action

Director’s intent is the moral, social, or political takeaway. Subtext is the unspoken meaning communicated beneath dialogue and action.

Visual characterisation

What bodies and clothes reveal

Costume, hair, makeup, posture, expression, eye contact, and vocal delivery indicate status, psychology, alignment, or transformation.

World-building

Designing social reality

Props, architecture, colour palette, set decoration, and spatial staging establish time, mood, intimacy, dominance, and social conditions.

Camera angles

Low anglePower, dominance, intimidation
High angleWeakness, fear, vulnerability
Canted / Dutch angleInstability and disorientation

Camera shots

Extreme close-upIntense emotion or meaningful detail
Close-upIntimacy and reaction
Medium shotCharacter interaction and body language

Camera movement

Tracking / dollyDraws the audience into motion
Pan / tiltReveals scale and context
Crane / zoomCreates isolation or connection
Lighting & colour

Shape the emotional temperature

High-key: brightness and soft shadows suggest openness, truth, security, or innocence.

Low-key / chiaroscuro: contrast and deep shadow suggest secrecy, fear, conflict, or moral ambiguity.

Colour: warm tones can signal passion, danger, nostalgia; cool tones can signal isolation, distance, melancholy.

Audio design

Hear the story world

Diegetic: heard inside the story world—dialogue, footsteps, environmental noise.

Non-diegetic: added outside it—score, narration, heightened effects.

Sound motif: recurring audio associated with a character, threat, or idea.

Editing & composition

Control attention and time

Mise-en-scène: all visual elements arranged within the frame.

Cross-cutting: concurrent scenes build tension or thematic contrast.

Cut rate: fast edits create urgency; slow edits build contemplation or discomfort.

04 • Unfamiliar text

A four-pass reading strategy

First establish what the text says. Then examine exactly how language shapes attitude, atmosphere, and audience response.

Poetry • Non-fiction • Prose
Step 1Unpack the prompt

Identify the focus: attitude shift, relationship, atmosphere, or change.

Step 2Active scan

Circle dynamic verbs, sensory adjectives, sound devices, and syntax.

Step 3Literal read

State plainly what is happening on the surface.

Step 4Deep analysis

Unpack implicit connotations and their effect.

Word choice & diction

Track emotional loading

  • Dynamic / imperative verbs: speed, force, urgency
  • Evaluative adjectives: signal emotional orientation
  • Connotation: emotional or cultural meaning beyond definition
Sound devices

Listen for pace and mood

  • Alliteration: repeated initial consonants
  • Assonance: repeated internal vowel sounds
  • Onomatopoeia: words that echo real sound
Structure & rhetoric

Notice shape and address

  • Short sentences: shock or emphasis
  • Extended syntax: reflection or overwhelm
  • Rhetorical questions: provoke alignment
  • Hyperbole: dramatic or satirical impact
  • “We”, “us”, “you”: solidarity or challenge
05 • Essay formulas

Architecture before decoration

Plan the job of each section, then choose evidence and language that complete that job.

Introduction → Body → Conclusion
Formula
Context + Text details + Thesis + Roadmap
1Context / HookA broad statement about the central topic.
2Text detailsTitle, author/director, minimal relevant context.
3ThesisDirect answer: what the creator shows and why.
4RoadmapThe key points or techniques you will unpack.
P.E.E.L. / T.E.E.A.L.
Point + Evidence + Explanation / Analysis + Link
PPoint“In [text], [creator] uses [feature] to demonstrate [argument].”
EEvidence“This is illustrated when [context], ‘[precise detail]’.”
AExplanation & AnalysisName the feature, then unpack literal meaning, connotation, and audience response.
LLink & PurposeConnect to the broader theme and an insight about society or human nature.
Formula
Restated thesis + Synthesis + Final “so what?”
1Restated thesisRephrase your overall argument in fresh language.
2Summary of pointsSynthesise—not list—the key ideas and techniques.
3Final insightEnd on creator intent or the text’s enduring relevance.
